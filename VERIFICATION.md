### Product tenets at stage completion

Each implementation stage must demonstrate the following through its completed caller flow, alongside its correctness and recovery checks:

- **Simplicity:** explain the required behavior or demonstrated cost that justifies each added module, durable fact, cache or queue. Check for duplicate authority and caller-owned coordination that belongs inside an existing owner; compare a simpler approach where one could preserve the required behavior. Identify dependencies introduced or removed on another owner's representation, call order or lifetime; justify each retained dependency by required behavior. Passing tests establishes tested behavior, not design simplicity.
- **Explainability:** walk ordinary work, failure and recovery in plain language using the stage's normal results and inspection. A reader should be able to tell what happened, what remains uncertain and what can happen next. Explanations must follow owning facts without treating optional diagnostics as recovery authority. Check this as each caller path appears, before final presentation work.
- **Memory footprint:** rerun comparable fixtures as resource owners are added, recording aggregate peak and retained-idle footprint, including native libraries and Rui helpers. At fixed capacity, vary payload and durable-history populations independently and exercise fill/drain with delayed cleanup or failure. Explain changes from the preceding measurement and resolve unexplained growth before dependent work builds on it. Use the existing [resource measurements and targets](#resources-and-accepted-host-qualification-targets); stage checks do not replace qualification of the complete simultaneous workload or introduce per-module quotas.

Record these checks with the stage's existing evidence; no separate scorecard or reporting system is required.
