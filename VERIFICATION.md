### Product tenets at stage completion

Each implementation stage must demonstrate the following through its completed caller flow, alongside its correctness and recovery checks:

- **Simplicity:** explain the required behavior or demonstrated cost that justifies each added module, durable fact, cache or queue. Check for duplicate authority and caller-owned coordination that belongs inside an existing owner; compare a simpler approach where one could preserve the required behavior. Identify dependencies introduced or removed on another owner's representation, call order or lifetime; justify each retained dependency by required behavior. Passing tests establishes tested behavior, not design simplicity.
- **Explainability:** walk ordinary work, failure and recovery in plain language using the stage's normal results and inspection. A reader should be able to tell what happened, what remains uncertain and what can happen next. Explanations must follow owning facts without treating optional diagnostics as recovery authority. Check this as each caller path appears, before final presentation work.
- **Memory footprint:** rerun comparable fixtures as resource owners are added, recording aggregate peak and retained-idle footprint, including native libraries and Rui helpers. At fixed capacity, vary payload and durable-history populations independently and exercise fill/drain with delayed cleanup or failure. Explain changes from the preceding measurement and resolve unexplained growth before dependent work builds on it. Use the existing [resource measurements and targets](#resources-and-accepted-host-qualification-targets); stage checks do not replace qualification of the complete simultaneous workload or introduce per-module quotas.

Record these checks with the stage's existing evidence; no separate scorecard or reporting system is required.

### Scratch and cleanup

Verify **8 GiB** default aggregate logical scratch using smaller exhaustion fixtures across requests, captures/metadata, Edit, ingress, reports/exports and spillover. Reserve growth before I/O; cover overflow, sparse gaps, partial writes, overwrites, truncation, ownership transfer and concurrent reservations without double/refunded charges. Include real disk-full, empty-file/handle populations, failed cleanup and protected I/O. FIFO reclaims oldest eligible spillover only after its canonical terminal result is saved and execution releases it; failure/all-protected cases cannot oversubscribe or spin. External readers may retain removed files and mutate published paths outside Host accounting; report that physical usage honestly. No reuse of saved output paths, hidden reader tracking or replay after loss.

Ingress plain and escaped representations charge equal decoded growth. A full budget rejects a crossing write before I/O; read-only content custody retains its charge until final close. Failed unlink retains the affected file charge, while unrelated content cleanup releases its own charge. Let Bash remove its own private script name, then require continuation, custody release and exactly-once accounting after the owner closes its descriptor. Unknown partial writes retain the submitted increment. These are bounded ownership tests, not large-payload performance qualification.

Verify exactly one cleanup owner at every resource handoff, including failed handoff. For each temporary owner, count files, descriptors, tracking records and logical bytes separately at preparation, seal, handoff, commit, semantic completion and final cleanup. Cover empty files, pending opens, overlapping copies, failed shrink/close/remove and delayed callbacks; measure delayed cleanup against monotonic elapsed time while independently varying idle-loop cadence and scheduler oversleep. No reported release without established reclamation, no double charge/refund on ownership transfer, and no replacement-allocation or cleanup spin. Retained cleanup failures must consume capacity or stop the Host and prevent unsafe startup, never disappear from accounting. For each temporary owner, crash after its first acquisition and across ownership handoffs, including the first streaming request while `request-0-1.tmp` or `report-0-1.tmp` is named.
