# Supported curl adjustments: source evidence

Checked 2026-09-11 against the experimental curl 8.22.0 source and current official manuals. This note supports adjustment experiments; it does not establish measured bounds, durable control behavior or production qualification. [capture-sources.md](capture-sources.md) retains callback/pause/reuse evidence; [sources.md](sources.md) retains baseline allocation ownership.

## Receive credit and rate limiting

There is no independent public HTTP/2 receive-window option in the pinned [easy option declarations](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/include/curl/curl.h) or [multi option declarations](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/include/curl/multi.h). Window constants and adjustments live inside [http2.c](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/http2.c#L58). Changing those constants would be a client fork, not a supported setting. `CURLOPT_BUFFERSIZE` changes a preferred transfer buffer, not these HTTP/2 stream credits.

The supported knob with a direct pinned HTTP/2 credit effect is [CURLOPT_MAX_RECV_SPEED_LARGE](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLOPT_MAX_RECV_SPEED_LARGE.html). Its API meaning is average download rate in bytes/second, allowing initial/burst deviation; zero disables it. [setopt.c 2773–2785](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/setopt.c#L2773) accepts every nonnegative value, initializes the limiter with that rate and a burst cap of `max(rate, 32768)`. There is **no 8 KiB/s minimum accepted rate**. The 8 KiB value below is an initial HTTP/2 credit floor.

Pinned behavior:

- [http2.c 202–213](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/http2.c#L202): initial stream credit is 65,536 bytes unless positive limiter tokens per step are lower; then it is `max(tokens_per_step, 8192)`. Before each request, [2117–2125](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/http2.c#L2117) updates the connection's initial-window SETTINGS if necessary. Mixed per-transfer rates on one connection therefore also interact through these SETTINGS; uniform-rate experiments are easier to interpret.
- [http2.c 284–309](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/http2.c#L284): desired ongoing stream credit is zero while write-paused/failed; otherwise it follows currently available limiter tokens, or 10 MiB when unlimited. The initial 8 KiB floor is not reapplied in this function. Already advertised credit cannot simply be recalled by pausing; see the existing capture note.
- [ratelimit.c 33–74](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/ratelimit.c#L33): tokens replenish in discrete time steps and are capped by the burst allowance. [164 onward](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/ratelimit.c#L164) starts with one second's configured tokens. Known total size can tune step length/token count; do not equate tokens per step with configured bytes/second in every response. At 8 or 16 KiB/s an active but idle unknown-length transfer can accumulate up to 32 KiB tokens, rather than only one rate unit.
- [ratelimit.c 312 onward](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/ratelimit.c#L312): blocking sets tokens to zero; unblocking restarts the limiter, so time spent explicitly paused does not accumulate extra tokens. Repeated pause/unpause resets are consequently not proof that observed long-term throughput equals the configured nominal rate.
- [multi.c 1985–2033](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/multi.c#L1985): exhausted credit changes the transfer to rate-limiting state and schedules a timer; it does not sleep inside this function. The owner must continue driving timers, polling and controls. [ratelimit.c 260 onward](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/ratelimit.c#L260) includes accumulated token debt in its wait estimate.

Inference for experiments: a fixed rate can constrain credit much more tightly than unlimited reception, but it also throttles provider output and can accumulate delivery delay when output exceeds it. This is not capture-driven credit or a complete transport-memory bound: account for HTTP/2 queues/pools, TLS, handles, connections and the capture queue separately. Measure full bytes, sustained throughput, resumed delivery and pending output. Never present a lower rate's memory result without its elapsed-time cost.

## Partitioning streams without reducing the active population

[CURLMOPT_MAX_CONCURRENT_STREAMS](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLMOPT_MAX_CONCURRENT_STREAMS.html) defaults to 100 and accepts 1 through 2^31−1. [url.c 687–705](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/url.c#L687) independently checks the client's per-connection cap and the connection's server limit before reusing a busy connection. [http2.c 1179–1191](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/http2.c#L1179) records the peer's SETTINGS limit; [2730–2736](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/http2.c#L2730) exposes it to connection selection. The client setting also populates outgoing SETTINGS, but does not command the server to advertise the same limit.

[CURLMOPT_MAX_HOST_CONNECTIONS](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLMOPT_MAX_HOST_CONNECTIONS.html) limits simultaneously open connections per hostname/port (or proxy); exhausted capacity queues new transfers, including their timeout clocks. Thus client stream cap 100 and host/total connection capacity at least 10 can accommodate 1,000 simultaneous requests when the server permits at least 100 streams per connection. This is supported capacity arithmetic, **not a guarantee of ten established connections or 1,000 simultaneously active requests**. A server cap of 50 requires at least 20 connections for the same population. Keeping a single connection while lowering the cap to 100 reduces active capacity and is not an equivalent experiment.

Prove partitioning with server-observed simultaneous live requests, per-connection occupancy, connection count and completed full-byte capture. Count of attached easy handles includes pending work and is insufficient. Use a fixture barrier that waits until all requests are admitted before producing/completing responses, so short transfers cannot conceal a serial population. Distinguish a server-advertised cap from a client-local cap in the case names/results.

[CURLOPT_PIPEWAIT](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLOPT_PIPEWAIT.html) can wait for a connection's multiplexing capability to become known, reducing speculative connections at the cost of startup waiting. It does not provide reserved slots, exact connection partitioning or permission to exceed the peer's limit. More connections multiply TLS/socket and per-connection pool costs; lowering streams per connection alone leaves per-stream receive credit unchanged.

## Buffer and idle-cache adjustments

[CURLOPT_BUFFERSIZE](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLOPT_BUFFERSIZE.html) requests a receive-buffer size between 1,024 and 10 MiB, default 16 KiB; it is not an exact callback-size guarantee. Since 8.7.0 one transfer buffer is shared by a multi's easy handles. Set it before transfers; changing it on an active transfer is explicitly discouraged. A 1 KiB comparison can establish callback/scheduling cost but cannot by itself demonstrate reduced HTTP/2 credit or all transport buffers.

[CURLMOPT_MAXCONNECTS](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLMOPT_MAXCONNECTS.html) controls retained connections after use; zero chooses the dynamic default, not an empty cache. Compare a cache that retains the measured active connection population against a smaller explicit cache. Neither setting reduces the allowed active population; `MAX_HOST_CONNECTIONS`/`MAX_TOTAL_CONNECTIONS` govern that separately. Report active peak, post-completion, settled idle and multi-cleanup phases. A small cache may save retained pools but incur reconnect/handshake cost on the next burst; graceful shutdown may also retain state temporarily. Keep the multi for a fair TLS-session-cache comparison and separately count reused sockets, resumed TLS sessions and full handshakes as described in the capture source note.

These knobs merit measured combinations of fixed receive rate, connection partition and idle cache. None of the API evidence promises complete provider output, responsive **durable** controls and a given total-memory ceiling together. If those coexist in an experiment, document its rate/response/concurrency envelope and distinguish a fixture control transaction from the runtime's recovery-qualified acknowledgement path.

## Cold-start caveat: advertised capacity can arrive after assignment

A peer cap smaller than the local cap does not prove the first burst will spread automatically. [http2.c 2414–2416](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/http2.c#L2414) initially sets the connection's presumed maximum to the local multi stream cap. A burst can therefore attach many transfers before the peer's SETTINGS are processed. [http2.c 2133–2147](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/http2.c#L2133) submits requests to nghttp2; an assigned stream ID is accepted as submission success. The [nghttp2 API documentation](https://nghttp2.org/documentation/nghttp2_submit_request.html) explicitly distinguishes assigned IDs from streams actually created/sent.

In pinned [nghttp2 1.70.0 session.c 2315 and 2346](https://github.com/nghttp2/nghttp2/blob/v1.70.0/lib/nghttp2_session.c#L2315), opening queued streams is conditional on the outgoing concurrency maximum. Its [request-allowed check at 1440](https://github.com/nghttp2/nghttp2/blob/v1.70.0/lib/nghttp2_session.c#L1440) checks client/session/ID/GOAWAY state, not availability below the concurrent stream limit. A successful curl submission or verbose OPENED message consequently does not prove the server received that request.

On receiving the peer cap curl signals connection change, but [multi.c 1754–1763](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/multi.c#L1754) describes retrying connection-pending transfers; it does not migrate already assigned nghttp2 requests to new connections. Source-backed inference: local cap 1,000 and later peer cap 100 can leave 100 on-wire requests plus 900 internally queued on the initial connection. A fixture waiting for all 1,000 requests before completing any then stalls even though the host cap allows ten connections. This is an admission-barrier failure, not evidence of byte corruption or slow capture.

Set the local cap to 100 before attaching the first burst to prevent this particular overassignment, or establish/observe the peer SETTINGS before assigning the bulk burst. Verify the result: local cap alignment cannot predict arbitrary future peer decreases and is not a general migration guarantee. Capture ordering of connection establishment, peer SETTINGS and stream submissions plus server-observed admission to distinguish this inference from a different fixture/scheduler bug. `PIPEWAIT=1` alone waits for multiplexing capability and is not documented as a barrier for final peer stream-cap knowledge.

OpenRouter's [performance-routing documentation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection) describes rolling five-minute model/provider throughput statistics and percentile preferences. Its 50 tokens/s example is a routing threshold, not a measured model speed. Search-indexed official [R1 page](https://openrouter.ai/deepseek/deepseek-r1/apps) showed 65 tokens/s and [Qwen3.7 Plus performance page](https://openrouter.ai/qwen/qwen3.7-plus/performance) showed 13 tokens/s (15 average for Alibaba Cloud Int.), but direct retrieval did not expose those dynamic values. Treat those as cached indexed observations with unknown measurement time, **not confirmed-current model measurements**; they are not needed for the recommendations below.

For calibration only, four UTF-8 bytes per generated token is an explicit English-like content assumption, not a conversion law. Language, tokenizer, code, escaping and token boundaries change it. Under that assumption, 50/100/1,000 tokens/s imply 200/400/4,000 **content** bytes/s per stream, before SSE/JSON and HTTP/TLS overhead. Input tokens affect prefill/first-token latency; do not multiply input token throughput into response bytes. Hidden reasoning tokens may consume compute without equivalent transmitted text, while visible reasoning, tool arguments and final events add different transmitted content.

Recommended synthetic offered-response profiles, retaining 1,000 active streams:

| Profile | Offered SSE body rate per stream | Aggregate at 1,000 | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Slower | 1,024 B/s | 1,024,000 B/s | Long-lived low-rate streams; sparse callbacks. |
| Normal | 4,096 B/s | 4,096,000 B/s | Ordinary streaming plus explicit framing allowance. |
| Fast | 32,768 B/s | 32,768,000 B/s | Fast output with framing allowance; not a universal peak ceiling. |
| Optional very fast | 65,536 B/s | 65,536,000 B/s | Covers more headroom than the 1,000-token/s calibration. |

These are **test-design choices**, not provider-measured wire rates. The current 256-byte padded fixture records produce 4/16/128/256 events per second at these rates. As a separate illustrative mapping, if each 256-byte record represented eight four-byte tokens, normal and fast would represent about 128 and 1,024 tokens/s; the current literal `delta: fixture` does not implement that mapping and must not be reported as doing so. Measure exact body bytes offered and accepted; TLS/socket bytes are another layer. If exact provider realism is needed, obtain an authorized stream trace and preserve its event sizes/timing without assuming one token per event.

Keep separate cases for synchronized terminal bursts and delayed output released after a pause. Average token-generation figures do not bound those instantaneous arrivals. Continue full-byte/terminal-event checks; a limiter that is below offered SSE bytes changes workload delivery even when its number appears above the decoded-text byte estimate. At each configured rate record actual achieved throughput and elapsed time, since flow control, slow capture and fixture scheduling can lower achieved rate.

## Asymmetric staged uploads and timing

Pinned curl 8.22.0 declares `CURLINFO_POSTTRANSFER_TIME_T` and `CURLOPT_UPLOAD_BUFFERSIZE` in [curl.h](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/include/curl/curl.h#L3020); these are supported APIs, not assumptions from a newer client. [UPLOAD_BUFFERSIZE](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLOPT_UPLOAD_BUFFERSIZE.html) supports a requested minimum 16 KiB, default 64 KiB, maximum 2 MiB. Set before activation. Allocation is on demand and the requested size is not guaranteed. Comparing 16 versus 64 KiB preserves staged-body streaming and full length; it changes buffering, not context content. Include protocol/TLS queues and callback scratch beyond this buffer when interpreting memory.

Keep these timestamps distinct (all curl values are microseconds from transfer start and accumulate across followed redirects):

| Measurement | Meaning and limit |
| --- | --- |
| [PRETRANSFER_TIME_T](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLINFO_PRETRANSFER_TIME_T.html) | Protocol setup/negotiations complete and transfer about to begin; not upload completion. |
| [POSTTRANSFER_TIME_T](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLINFO_POSTTRANSFER_TIME_T.html) | Last byte sent by libcurl. Not proof the remote application received, validated or durably recorded the body. A userspace relay can make that distinction particularly large. |
| [STARTTRANSFER_TIME_T](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLINFO_STARTTRANSFER_TIME_T.html) | First response byte received by libcurl, including preceding setup/server delay. Not necessarily the first SSE text token; headers or an interim response can precede useful model output. |
| Fixture body-complete / first parsed output event | Independently record server full-body length/hash completion and client first semantic SSE delta. These prove the corresponding application boundaries, rather than inferring them from curl timing names. |

Report transfer-start-to-first-semantic-output latency as the caller's observed first-output delay. `posttransfer - pretransfer` is an upload-phase approximation; `first_output - posttransfer` includes remaining network/relay delivery, server admission/prefill and return delivery. Neither subtraction isolates model computation. Retain raw stamps and detect early responses instead of forcing negative phase differences to zero. The final SSE event, transport completion and capture completion remain separate.

Use growing staged inputs followed by a smaller compacted input, with exact per-step byte sizes reported; an illustrative 100:1 request/response size ratio is one case, not a workload invariant. [OpenAI prompt-caching documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching) describes reuse of server-side KV state for a matching incoming prefix. Inference: that cache hit alone does not remove the serialized prefix from a self-contained HTTP request. Stateful continuation APIs are a separate capability and are not enabled by this experiment. The same documentation explains that compaction replaces earlier context and can reduce prefix-cache reuse; fewer serialized input bytes and less server-side cache reuse can therefore occur together. Do not discount measured upload bytes because a provider might cache their computation.

An isolated userspace TCP relay can throttle/delay ciphertext forwarding without terminating TLS, but its two TCP connections terminate and acknowledge independently. It does not reproduce end-to-end packet RTT, congestion windows, retransmission, loss, ACK pacing, MTU or kernel queue behavior of a WAN/netem setup. Call its controls **forwarding delay and ciphertext byte rate**, not measured packet RTT/bandwidth. TCP reads can split or combine TLS records; forwarding read chunks is not automatically record shaping. Per-connection rate limits also multiply with connection count unless a shared aggregate budget is implemented.

[Python stream documentation](https://docs.python.org/3/library/asyncio-stream.html) specifies that writes may enter an internal buffer and `drain()` waits for high/low-watermark flow control, not remote receipt. Bound and account for relay read buffers, pending delayed chunks and write buffers; include its separate RSS where relevant while keeping it outside client memory. An unbounded delay queue would move the memory problem into the fixture. Explicitly yield when needed because a drain below its high watermark may return immediately. Measure effective throughput/delay and server body completion to expose buffering effects; these results remain userspace shaping evidence, not packet-network qualification.

The accepted no-automatic-replay behavior is unchanged. Failed/partial uploads and libcurl's internal reconnect/rewind paths still need transport qualification against the staged body and authority boundary before production adoption; successful upload-memory measurements do not supply that evidence or authorize replay.

## Per-socket send-buffer experiment

[CURLOPT_SOCKOPTFUNCTION](https://curl.se/libcurl/c/CURLOPT_SOCKOPTFUNCTION.html) is the supported hook for setting socket options after socket creation and before connect; callback user data comes from `CURLOPT_SOCKOPTDATA`. For this TCP experiment handle `CURLSOCKTYPE_IPCXN`, call `setsockopt(fd, SOL_SOCKET, SO_SNDBUF, ...)`, check its return and inspect the effective value with `getsockopt`. Return `CURL_SOCKOPT_OK` on success or `CURL_SOCKOPT_ERROR` on failure; do not return ALREADY_CONNECTED for a newly created socket. [Pinned cf-socket.c 1280 onward](https://github.com/curl/curl/blob/curl-8_22_0/lib/cf-socket.c#L1280) calls the hook and aborts the connection attempt on callback error. Reused connections do not constitute newly created sockets: report actual callback/new-socket counts and keep any callback state alive until its owner is finished.

[Apple's socket-option reference](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/System/Conceptual/ManPages_iPhoneOS/man2/setsockopt.2.html) identifies SO_SNDBUF as output-buffer sizing and documents setting/reading options. Requested bytes, observed effective buffer size and current occupied bytes are different quantities. Neither requested nor observed capacity is a measurement of resident kernel allocation or process RSS; do not add `socket_count * SO_SNDBUF` to RSS as measured memory. Report that product only as aggregate configured capacity, alongside process measurements and any separately measured kernel usage. Platform accounting/limits require their own verification, including Linux.

This setting targets socket send buffering and possible aggregate kernel pressure across many HTTP/1.1 connections. It does not reduce HTTP/2 receive credit or establish a transport-wide memory bound. A reported ENOBUFS send failure motivates a controlled comparison but alone does not identify the exhausted kernel pool. Preserve the original failure, complete-body hashes, concurrency and all failed cases when comparing outcomes; a smaller buffer that succeeds is evidence for that tested workload, not a general cure.

The userspace relay still acknowledges its client-side TCP locally. Successful small-SO_SNDBUF throughput through that relay therefore does not qualify real-WAN bandwidth-delay-product behavior: delayed forwarding is not delayed TCP ACKs. A smaller send buffer may constrain throughput over a genuine high-RTT path, so that tradeoff remains unmeasured without packet-level or real-path evidence. No retry/replay policy changes follow from the experiment.
